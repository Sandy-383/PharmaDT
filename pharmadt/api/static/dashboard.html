—

What each number means, and which ones are honest to quote.

Service level and stockout

Stockout rate is the fraction of demand that could not be met from stock on hand. Service level is its complement. These are the headline operational metrics, and the agents' clearest win: stockout falls by about 86% against a no-agent baseline, averaged over ten seeds.

They are reported together because either alone is easy to game — you can drive stockout to zero by holding unlimited inventory, which is why average inventory sits in the same table.

Wastage, and the control that matters

Wastage is units expired before use. The reduction is measured against a no-redistribution control — the full agent stack with the Expiry Agent removed — not against the no-agent baseline.

That distinction is the single most important methodological point in the project. The no-agent baseline wastes very little, but only because it holds less stock and stocks out far more often. Comparing wastage across two policies running at different service levels measures the service level, not the redistribution. Both comparisons are printed; only the controlled one is the claim.

Forecast error: sMAPE, not MAPE

MAPE is undefined when actual demand is zero, which at a pharmacy happens constantly. sMAPE and MASE are reported alongside it for that reason. MASE is scaled so that 1.00 is exactly the seasonal-naive baseline — below 1.00 beats it.

Read the shared protocol before the broad sweep. On identical windows, a moving average and the ensemble both edge out the LSTM; the LSTM's larger margin on the broad sweep comes from different windows against a weaker baseline. The defensible claim is that it beats the seasonal baseline comfortably — not that it beats every classical method.

Detection: why accuracy is absent

Anomalies are about 5% of shipments. A detector that flags nothing scores 95% accuracy and catches no counterfeits, so accuracy is the trap here. Precision, recall, F1 and ROC-AUC are the columns that carry information.

One injected anomaly type — counterfeit — deliberately changes nothing observable about the shipment. It is undetectable from shipment features alone and is caught by the ledger's fingerprint check instead. That is the argument for pairing ML with cryptographic provenance rather than relying on either alone.

Privacy: the rows that look bad

The differential-privacy regimes at ε = 1 destroy the forecast, and those rows are shown rather than dropped. At this dataset size that is the real privacy/utility trade-off, and the honest finding is that meaningful privacy costs meaningful accuracy here.

Noise is calibrated to the clipped update and divided by the client count, because it is applied to the mean rather than the sum — an earlier version added noise N times too large.
